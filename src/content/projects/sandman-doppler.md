I founded Palo Alto Innovation in 2015 and ran it for nine years. It was bootstrapped, with no investors, and grew to about ten people: industrial design, electrical and mechanical engineering, firmware, software, mobile and sales. I hired all of them and they reported to me.

## The clock

The Sandman Doppler is a smart alarm clock that runs Linux and has Alexa built in. I came up with the concept, drew the first sketch and the app wireframes, and ran the crowdfunding campaigns, including Kickstarter.

From there I oversaw everything it takes to make a consumer electronics product real: development, tooling, production at overseas contract manufacturers, import, logistics, the website, marketing and sales. We sold thousands of units through our own site and Amazon, and I stayed involved in supporting customers after the sale.

## The redesign nobody wants

Partway through, the supplier of our system-on-module went out of business. That module was the heart of the product, so losing it meant re-architecting the whole thing. I found and qualified the replacement, and the redesign took more than a year. We still delivered.

The same years included plenty of chip shortages and single-source parts, which taught me to care a lot about second sources.

## PAI Labs

Alongside our own products we ran PAI Labs, a design firm that took client projects across many industries and built dozens of prototypes and proofs of concept. One client was NASA, and something we built is on the International Space Station.
