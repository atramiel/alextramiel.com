Hyphen builds custom robotic food makelines for restaurant operators. I joined in August 2024 as Operations Manager and was promoted to Head of Operations in June 2026, when I took on all of manufacturing, procurement, PLM and BOM management. I reported to the founders and picked up whatever needed an owner.

## Buying and suppliers

I ordered the parts for every Hyphen product on a budget of about $1M. That meant more than 1,000 made-to-spec and off-the-shelf parts, in nested assemblies that sometimes ran seven levels deep.

About 20 main suppliers, foreign and domestic, dealt with me as their main contact. I negotiated pricing, chased lead times, sent defects back from engineering, audited factories in person and hired overseas auditors when I couldn't be there. Several suppliers I found became pivotal, including the one we custom-developed our actuators with.

## Manufacturing

I hired and supervised the manufacturing technicians, ran multiple production ramps, and helped move and redesign the production area. I set up and maintained the engineering change process, with changes flowing from Onshape to Arena to NetSuite once they took effect.

A lot of the gains were small and unglamorous. Commonizing screws and removing leftover steps shortened build time. Reviewing and redlining drawings caught problems before they became parts. Twice we worked with engineering on a 3D-printing finishing process that was new to the market.

## Getting machines to customers

All freight went through me, from parcel to full truckload to white-glove delivery. I selected and onboarded a 3PL, designed a reusable shipping crate to cut logistics costs, and supported more than a dozen customer installs, including site surveys and work on site.

## IT and the building

After canceling our managed service provider I was the whole IT department for 35 people: accounts, laptops, security, Wi-Fi, door access and a site-to-site VPN between locations. I also instrumented the office with sensors and presence-based automations.

Together with getting us out of a bad storage arrangement, those changes saved the company about $158K a year.

## Demos

I planned and ran the live demos for customers, vendors and investors, with production cameras, microphones and a video mixer. A webhook integration with the machine drove a real-time overlay on the stream.

## Meals instead of waste

Testing food robots produces a lot of food. I pushed for the food safety practices and built the labeling tool we needed to donate it, which turned into thousands of meals for a local shelter.
